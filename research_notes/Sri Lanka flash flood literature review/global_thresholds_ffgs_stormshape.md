## Q1. Rainfall thresholds for flash floods: ID/ED thresholds, antecedent conditioning, ROC optimisation, Bayesian and frequentist methods, model-based and satellite-based thresholds

### Takeaway
Flash flood thresholds have grown from fixed accumulations over set durations into conditional and multi-variable schemes. These include thresholds tied to soil moisture or antecedent rain, dual "depth + peak intensity" thresholds, thresholds that depend on the rainfall pattern, and FFG-type thresholds from hydrological models, usually tuned with ROC or contingency-table scores. The most rigorous statistical machinery (frequentist ED thresholds with bootstrap uncertainty, CTRL-T) comes from landslide research and has seen little use for flash floods. In China, the most explicit attempts to bring rainfall pattern into thresholds use mostly model-derived critical rainfall per pattern class, not a pattern response estimated from observations.

### Cited Findings

**Reviews / framing**
- A 2024 literature review (Water Practice & Technology 19(11):4486) makes two points. First, threshold sensitivity to antecedent soil conditions tends to fall as rainfall duration grows. Second, the temporal pattern of rainfall becomes more important to the threshold over the same range. It also notes that the US NWS Flash Flood Guidance issues warnings from soil-moisture-dependent rainfall thresholds — [IWA review](https://iwaponline.com/wpt/article/19/11/4486/105548/A-literature-review-rainfall-thresholds-as-flash)
- Georgakakos (2006) defines rainfall thresholds as "the rainfall conditions for a given duration and initial soil moisture status that when reached or exceeded, are likely to trigger a flash flood". An earlier Georgakakos-group finding is that precipitation is the dominant source of uncertainty when using thresholds to assess flood risk (as reported in search snippets citing those works) — [Georgakakos 2006, J. Hydrol. 317:81–103, doi:10.1016/j.jhydrol.2005.05.009](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222571010_Analytical_results_for_operational_flash_flood_guidance)
- The search also returned a methodological comparison of flood rainfall-threshold estimation methods in Natural Hazards (2014/15). Its authors and results were not retrieved — [Comparison of methodologies for flood rainfall thresholds estimation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-014-1357-3)

**Soil-moisture / antecedent-conditioned thresholds (Europe, landmark)**
- Norbiato, Borga et al. (2008, J. Hydrol.) set flash flood warning thresholds as a function of rainfall and soil moisture, and tested them in gauged and ungauged basins (northeast Italy). This is the canonical "FFG-like, soil-moisture-conditioned threshold" paper in Europe — [Norbiato et al. 2008, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169408004587); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222568503_Flash_flood_warning_based_on_rainfall_thresholds_and_soil_moisture_conditions_An_assessment_for_gauged_and_ungauged_basins)
- One study found that the rainfall threshold falls significantly as antecedent soil moisture rises in humid regions, but only slightly in semi-humid and semi-arid regions (snippet; specific paper not resolved) — [search summary of threshold literature, see IWA review and linked works](https://iwaponline.com/wpt/article/19/11/4486/105548/A-literature-review-rainfall-thresholds-as-flash)
- A 2025 Natural Hazards study compared three soil-moisture-based threshold methods in two small mountain catchments. All three reached "good accuracy (Hit Rate (HR) > 0.80, False Alarm Rate (FAR) < 0.20)" using gauge data — [Natural Hazards 2025, s11069-025-07272-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-025-07272-6)
- Bayesian decision approach: the search returned a chapter titled "Rainfall Thresholds for Flood Warning Systems: A Bayesian Decision Approach". It is likely by Martina/Todini et al. (attribution unverified) — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-77843-1_9)
- Real-time threshold approach for complex geomorphological basins (Crete, Greece; Kourgialas, Karatzas, Nikolaidis) — [PDF](https://ermis-f.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Development-of-a-thresholds-approach-for-real-timeflash-flood-prediction-in-complex-geomorphologicalriver-basins-N.-Kourgialas-G.-P.-Karatzas-N.-P.-Nikolaidis.pdf)

**ROC / contingency-table optimisation**
- Post-wildfire ID thresholds (southern California, NHESS 2022) used ROC analysis following Staley et al. (2013), scoring each candidate threshold by threat score (TS = CSI). The highest TS were "generally associated with the 70th–95th percentiles". I30 thresholds rose from 16.0 mm/h in post-fire year 1 to 34.5 mm/h in year 5, so these thresholds change over time — [NHESS 22:361, 2022](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/22/361/2022/)
- Korea (Han River basin, NHESS 2018): ROC-optimised precipitation–area curves for short-duration flash flood warnings gave generalised thresholds of 42, 32 and 20 mm/h for sub-basins of 22–40, 40–100 and >100 km² respectively. The method is "based on FFG analysis to avoid the need to estimate soil moisture conditions" — [NHESS 18:171, 2018](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/18/171/2018/)
- US verification practice shows the usual trade-off: low thresholds give high POD but also high FAR. The NOAA "precipitation proxies" work optimises ETS against flash flood reports, and a 2021 comment reports strong regional dependence of the best-performing average recurrence intervals — [NOAA repository, 7-year proxies study](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65900/noaa_65900_DS1.pdf); [JHM 2021 comment](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/hydr/22/3/JHM-D-20-0215.1.xml)

**Frequentist ED thresholds and CTRL-T (landslide analogues)**
- CTRL-T (Melillo et al. 2018, Environmental Modelling & Software) is an open-source R tool. It rebuilds rainfall events and the rainfall conditions behind known failures from standard rain series, then fits power-law cumulated event rainfall–duration (ED) thresholds at chosen non-exceedance probabilities with bootstrap uncertainty. It follows the frequentist method of Brunetti et al. (2010), updated by Peruccacci et al. — [CNR-IRPI: How to define rainfall thresholds automatically](https://www.irpi.cnr.it/en/focus/how-to-define-rainfall-thresholds-automatically/); [Natural Hazards 2020 application](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11069-019-03830-x.pdf)
- Brunetti et al. (2010, NHESS), "Rainfall thresholds for the possible occurrence of landslides in Italy", is the landmark frequentist ID/ED threshold paper — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43047533_Rainfall_thresholds_for_the_possible_occurrence_of_landslides_in_Italy); Peruccacci et al. (2017) national update — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315973567_Rainfall_thresholds_for_possible_landslide_occurrence_in_Italy)
- Satellite thresholds in South Asia: HESS (2021) used CTRL-T on India and found that "satellite rainfall products outperform ground observations for landslide prediction in India" (products include GPM/IMERG; product-specific numbers not retrieved). This is the strongest precedent for satellite-based empirical ED thresholds in a monsoon, data-scarce setting — [HESS 25:3267, 2021](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/3267/2021/)
- Frequentist landslide thresholds for Darjeeling and Nilgiris (India) were presented at EGU 2023 — [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023EGUGA..2511853M/abstract)
- An enhanced rainfall-induced landslide catalogue for Italy (Scientific Data, 2025) shows the catalogue-building that frequentist thresholds depend on — [Nature Sci. Data 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04551-6)

**Hydrological-model-based thresholds and rainfall-pattern-conditioned thresholds (China)**
- China, distributed-model critical rainfall for mountainous areas: J. Hydrol. 2016 (authors likely Miao et al.; unverified) — [ScienceDirect S0022169416302475](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169416302475)
- A mountain-catchment threshold study took account of both antecedent soil moisture and rainfall pattern. It classed events as leading, intermediate or delayed by whether >40% of the rain fell in the first, middle or last third of the duration. It notes that "H1" is a widely used pattern recommended by Chinese rainstorm-statistics guidance (this is effectively a three-class Huff-type scheme; Huff is not named in the snippet) — [ResearchGate 326330474](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326330474_Rainfall_threshold_determination_for_flash_flood_warning_in_mountainous_catchments_with_consideration_of_antecedent_soil_moisture_and_rainfall_pattern)
- Yunnan (Ma et al. 2021, JFRM) proposed a new rainfall-triggering index. Related Yunnan work picked the "five most likely rainfall patterns" from the timing of the rain peak in historical events, then derived critical thresholds for each pattern × soil-moisture class — [Ma et al. 2021 JFRM](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfr3.12676)
- Sichuan-related results found that threshold uncertainty from rainfall pattern is greater than that from soil moisture. A Henan study with random rainfall patterns reports that critical rainfall has "great uncertainty, which causes missed alarms" (snippets) — [Frontiers Env. Sci. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2023.1221571/pdf); [Water Resour. Manage. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11269-022-03106-3)
- Dual threshold (Zhongdu River, Sichuan; Natural Hazards 2024) pairs cumulative depth with maximum intensity, because the runoff peak depends on rainfall pattern and a single critical accumulation is too uncertain. It reports an average lead time of 46.2 min and fewer false or missed warnings than single thresholds — [Natural Hazards 2024, s11069-024-06493-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-024-06493-5)
- A 2026 comparison of rainfall-based and discharge-based early warning (Water 18(1):64) notes that China's operational method uses fixed 1-, 3- and 6-h thresholds, which "may not fully accommodate the diverse temporal structures of rainfall events" — [Water 2026, doi:10.3390/w18010064](https://doi.org/10.3390/w18010064)

**Satellite-rainfall-based flash flood thresholds**
- Yunnan, China (Remote Sensing 2020, 12:1954): gauge-calibrated IMERG was used to derive static flash flood warning thresholds. Accuracy differs between near-real-time and Final runs — [doi:10.3390/rs12121954](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12121954)
- An IMERG-based global extreme-rainfall detection system tunes its thresholds against misses and false alarms. In very dry countries the thresholds come close to satellite measurement error; in very wet ones they can become unrealistically high — [ResearchGate 331943338](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331943338_Improving_an_Extreme_Rainfall_Detection_System_with_GPM_IMERG_data)
- Central Vietnam: a retrospective early-flood-warning case study combines IMERG with satellite convective-system indicators (arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed) — [arXiv 2403.14395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14395)
- Xijiang basin, southern China: GSMaP-Gauge detected ~90% of rain events at 1 mm/day, but more than half of ≥50 mm/day extremes were missed by satellite products. This bears directly on threshold exceedance detection — [Atmos. Res. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169809523000704)

### Inferences
- In tropical/monsoon settings the methods are not evenly developed. Landslide research has rigorous, reproducible empirical-threshold tools (CTRL-T, bootstrap ED thresholds, satellite-rain tests in India). Flash flood thresholds in Asia mostly come from models (critical rainfall from distributed models or FFG). A Sri Lankan study that transfers CTRL-T-style frequentist ED/ID fitting to a flash flood event inventory, and scores it with TSS/CSI/HSS, would fill a real methodological gap.
- The Chinese "rainfall-pattern-conditioned threshold" work is the closest precedent for combining thresholds with storm shape. In most of what I found, though, the pattern-specific thresholds come from model simulations with design or historical pattern classes. They are not estimated empirically from how observed events responded.
- Because thresholds change over time (wildfire) and vary by region (US ARIs), Sri Lankan thresholds should be stratified by climatic zone (wet/intermediate/dry) and monsoon season.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed flash flood (not landslide) threshold study using CTRL-T or the Brunetti/Peruccacci frequentist method. One may exist but did not appear.
- No country-specific flash flood threshold studies were found in this session for the Philippines, Nepal, Bangladesh, Brazil, Iran, Spain or Taiwan. These need targeted searches. Known landmark works outside this session (not verified here): Caine (1980) ID landslide thresholds; Guzzetti et al. (2007, 2008) threshold reviews; Javelle et al. AIGA (France); Alfieri & Thielen ERIC (Europe); Gourley et al. US flash flood database / FLASH.
- Exact skill scores for most of the Chinese threshold papers were not available from snippets.

## Q2. Flash Flood Guidance System (FFGS): concept, regional implementations, published verification and skill, known limitations

### Takeaway
FFGS (HRC with WMO, NOAA and USAID) gives every small basin a model-based rainfall threshold (flash flood guidance, FFG). FFG is computed in real time from a soil moisture model plus threshold runoff, and is compared with satellite/radar/gauge rainfall to produce flash flood threat. It runs operationally across many regions, including SAsiaFFGS, which covers Sri Lanka. However, peer-reviewed quantitative verification is thin and patchy. The best large-sample verification is from the US NWS FFG (CONUS, 2006–2010), not the HRC global FFGS. Published HRC-FFGS skill numbers are mostly single-country or event-based, and the HRC verification guidelines themselves stress uncertainty in the ground-truth data.

### Cited Findings
- Concept: Georgakakos (2006) gave the analytical basis for operational FFG: threshold runoff (the runoff volume that brings the outlet to bankfull) combined with real-time soil water deficit gives the rainfall depth over a given duration needed to cause flooding — [Georgakakos 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222571010_Analytical_results_for_operational_flash_flood_guidance). A companion result reports that FFG thresholds tend to give high POD where flash floods are frequent (snippet) — same source cluster.
- Worldwide implementation review: Georgakakos et al. (2022, BAMS 103(3)), "The Flash Flood Guidance System Implementation Worldwide: A Successful Multidecadal Research-to-Operations Effort". Per snippets, it says verification of FFGS products is "an important aspect of the implementation and subsequent operations". For Central America it says the designers judged that additional regional and local quality control of satellite precipitation would be necessary for beneficial results (full text not retrieved) — [BAMS 2022](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/103/3/BAMS-D-20-0241.1.xml)
- Regional systems listed by WMO: Black Sea and Middle East FFGS (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bulgaria, Georgia, Jordan, Lebanon, Israel, Türkiye as regional centre); Central America FFGS (Costa Rica regional centre; Belize, El Salvador as back-up centre, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, Panama); Southern Africa (South Africa regional centre); South Asia (India regional centre) — [WMO FFGS with Global Coverage](https://community.wmo.int/site/knowledge-hub/programmes-and-initiatives/hydrology-and-water-resources/flash-flood-guidance-system-ffgs-global-coverage); [HRC Global FFG programme](https://www.hrcwater.org/projects/project-1/)
- SAsiaFFGS: launched by IMD in October 2020 with WMO and HRC. It covers India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal and **Sri Lanka**, with threat guidance about 6 h ahead and risk about 24 h ahead — [IMD press release 2020](https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20201023_pr_922.pdf); [WMO SAsiaFFGS page](https://community.wmo.int/en/south-asia-flash-flood-guidance-system-sasiaffgs); [HRC training for South Asia](https://www.hrcwater.org/all-news/hrc-provides-flash-flood-guidance-system-training-for-south-asia-countries/)
- SAsiaFFGS evaluation (Himachal Pradesh, India, July 2023 event): compared FFGS risk and threat indices with observed rainfall, soil moisture evolution and satellite-derived runoff. It reported that the system captured the spatial and temporal evolution of flash flood conditions and gave usable lead time. This is a single event, qualitative per the snippet, and the venue was not confirmed — [ResearchGate 403537199](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403537199_Evaluating_the_performance_of_the_Flash_Flood_Guidance_System_A_Case_study_from_Himachal_Pradesh_India)
- Reported national skill (secondary source, attribution not checked): Croatia reported a skill score of 84% for 2016; the Turkish State Meteorological Service reported POD of 70% (2014) and 55% (2015) — [FloodList: WMO's FFGS improves forecasts and warnings](https://floodlist.com/protection/wmos-flash-flood-guidance-system-improves-forecasts-warnings) (figures came from a search summary; check against the original WMO/HRC reports before citing)
- HRC Technical Report No. 102 (2019), Verification Guidelines for FFGS: gives verification guidance for precipitation (estimated and forecast), soil water saturation fraction and flash flood threat, and stresses uncertainty in ground-truth flash flood observations — [HRC TR-102 PDF](http://www.hrcwater.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/HRC_TechnicalReport102_20190505.pdf)
- "Modern operational flash flood warning systems based on flash flood guidance theory: performance evaluation" is an HRC-group performance evaluation paper (authors, venue and numbers not retrieved) — [ResearchGate 262262961](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262262961_Modern_operational_flash_flood_warning_systems_based_on_flash_flood_guidance_theory_Performance_evaluation)
- An independent programme review exists for the WMO "FFGS with Global Coverage" project (UNEG) — [UN Evaluation Group](https://www.unevaluation.org/member_publications/review-flash-flood-guidance-system-ffgs-global-coverage-project)
- US NWS FFG verification (Clark, Gourley et al. 2014, Weather and Forecasting 29(2)): the first CONUS-wide verification, using Storm Data reports for 2006–2010, computed POD, FAR and CSI. It noted there was no system to feed back FFG effectiveness to WFOs/RFCs. Specific values not retrieved because the fetch failed — [WAF 2014](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/29/2/waf-d-12-00124_1.xml); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262955697_CONUS-Wide_Evaluation_of_National_Weather_Service_Flash_Flood_Guidance_Products). A GitHub repository cites CSI 0.00–0.44 for NOAA FFG; this is not peer-reviewed, so treat it as unverified — [GitHub](https://github.com/Mokshitavp1/flash_flood_warning_system)
- Gridded FFG validation (ABRFC poster) found that GFFG worked better for long storms than for short ones. This hints that FFG skill depends on storm temporal structure and duration — [Lakhankar poster](http://www.lakhankar.com/pubs/Poster_Juan_EPP_Forum_Florida.pdf)
- Uncertainty propagation: "Towards probabilistic forecasting of flash floods: the combined effects of uncertainty in radar-rainfall and flash flood guidance" (J. Hydrol. 2010; likely Villarini, Krajewski, Ntelekos, Georgakakos, Smith; authors unverified) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169410000843)
- Satellite precipitation bias is widely recognised as a major issue across basins worldwide. Satellite soil moisture covers only a shallow near-surface layer and has gaps under dense vegetation, which is relevant for tropical FFGS soil-moisture constraints — [Springer Applied Water Science 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13201-019-0931-y); [Reichle 2004 GRL](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2004gl020938)

### Inferences
- FFGS works on fixed durations (typically 1, 3 and 6 h, plus 24 h in some products) and does not represent within-event temporal structure, beyond what the soil moisture model integrates. The GFFG result (better for long than short storms) and the Chinese fixed-duration critique point the same way: duration-bucketed thresholds under-serve short, peaky convective bursts. That is exactly where storm shape could add information.
- No peer-reviewed, multi-year, contingency-table verification of SAsiaFFGS over Sri Lanka was found. A Sri Lankan threshold study that also scores FFGS threat outputs (if archives can be obtained from DoM/IMD) against a local flash flood inventory would be new.
- Known structural limitations, which the 2022 BAMS review and HRC guidelines appear to acknowledge but whose quantification was not retrieved: (i) satellite rainfall bias, especially underestimation of orographic and extreme rain; (ii) the soil moisture model (SAC-SMA-type) uncalibrated in ungauged basins; (iii) threshold runoff from bankfull geometry regionalised from sparse data; (iv) small-basin delineation (typically ~25–200 km² basins), which may not resolve very small steep catchments; (v) few ground-truth flash flood reports.

### Gaps
- I found no published POD/FAR/CSI for the Central America, Southern Africa, South Asia (beyond one event), Black Sea/Middle East or Southeast Asia (MRCFFG/SEAFFGS) systems from primary sources. The WMO steering committee material that holds verification presentations was not retrieved.
- The quantitative CONUS 2014 values (POD, FAR, CSI by product) need retrieval from the WAF paper.
- No information on SEAFFGS / Mekong FFGS verification was found.

## Q3. Storm temporal structure (shape, concentration, peak position) as a driver of flash flood occurrence and magnitude: model vs observed evidence; event-shape classification; shape in thresholds/EWS; changes with warming

### Takeaway
Model experiments clearly show that hyetograph shape changes peak discharge and time to peak, more so in small catchments and under dry antecedent conditions. Observational evidence exists but is much thinner and usually descriptive: case-based hydrograph styles, storm-core properties versus peaks, or attribution in large samples using daily data. **I found no study that systematically tests, across a large observed event inventory, whether hyetograph shape metrics (Huff quartile, peak position, Gini/concentration) separate flood from non-flood events, or improve threshold skill (TSS/CSI) beyond depth and duration.** On warming, observations show storms becoming steeper (Wasko & Sharma 2015) and more front-loaded (Visser et al. 2023) in Australia. Comparable tropical South Asian analyses were not found.

### Cited Findings

**Model-based / design-storm sensitivity**
- Zhu et al. (2018, WRR), "The impact of rainfall space-time structure in flood frequency analysis": a stochastic rainfall generator with a hillslope-resolving model simulated large samples of flood peaks. Under dry soil conditions, the contribution of rainfall temporal structure drops sharply as drainage area grows, except in the smallest catchments (<20 km²). Soil moisture changes how much structure matters — [Zhu et al. 2018 WRR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018WR023550)
- HESS 2021 (urban watershed; stochastic storm transposition + hydrological model): for extremes, peaks depended mainly on the spatial coverage of the storm core, and rainfall spatial heterogeneity raised peaks by 50% on average. For smaller, more frequent events, peaks followed temporal features (total rainfall, peak rainfall rate) — [HESS 25:4701, 2021](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/4701/2021/)
- Catalogue of reasonable worst-case rainfall series for flash flood simulation of short convective storms (J. Hydrol. 2024): short-triangle events gave the fastest time to peak and double-peak events the slowest. Long-triangle events gave the highest peak discharge and water depth. So the shape that maximises the peak is not the one that gives the fastest rise — [J. Hydrol. 2024, S0022169424004864](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169424004864)
- Seawell et al. (2025, J. Flood Risk Management), "Analysing flash flood hydrographs from different rainfall temporal profiles": short storms (1–4 h) were mostly sharper-peaked and more often front- or back-loaded, while long storms (6–24 h) were flatter and more centred. The full text, including whether hydrographs were observed or modelled, was not retrieved — [JFRM 2025, doi:10.1111/jfr3.70133](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfr3.70133)
- Máca & Torfs (2009, Soil & Water Research) linked a runoff model to stochastic rainfall disaggregation and stated that temporal distribution shapes the hydrograph, peak discharge and volume — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/695aec92946b4694bf5a9da8404d4afb)
- Chinese critical-rainfall-by-peak-position results: a figure titled "Critical rainfall with different rainfall peak positions and shape characteristics" shows the threshold (critical rainfall) varying with peak position, in a model-based design — [ResearchGate figure 353012632](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Critical-rainfall-with-different-rainfall-peak-positions-and-shape-characteristics-of-the_fig7_353012632)

**Observational evidence linking rainfall structure to observed flood response**
- Patagonia ephemeral streams (Kaless et al. 2024, WRR): from observed rainfall and hydrographs, the number and sequence of high-intensity periods largely set the number of hydrograph peaks. Typical events had high-intensity rain at the start followed by weaker sub-events. This is observational, but in a dryland setting — [WRR 2024, 2024WR037601](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024WR037601)
- HESS discussion paper hess-2018-550, "Contrasting rainfall–runoff characteristics of floods in [desert and Mediterranean basins]" (title truncated in result; Eastern Mediterranean, likely Rinat, Marra, Morin et al., unverified): 5,534 floods with radar rainfall. Storm-core properties related well to peak discharge, whereas mean catchment rainfall was a poor predictor because rainfall was strongly concentrated. This is an observational large sample, mostly about spatial concentration and storm cores — [HESS preprint PDF](https://hess.copernicus.org/preprints/hess-2018-550/hess-2018-550-manuscript-version2.pdf)
- Small agricultural catchment event-runoff variability (PMC 2021): an observational event-runoff study (details not retrieved) — [PMC7872197](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7872197/)
- Typhoon Lekima (Shandong, China): flood-peak responses to land use under extreme rain in small catchments (observational case study) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095633921000745)
- A search summary described a large-sample attribution over 140 catchments (304–86,475 km²) in the Eastern Monsoon Region of China using daily flood events. Antecedent wetness was the strongest control on peak variability (attributed CV 0.23), then temporal rainfall structure (CV 0.16), then spatial structure, and temporal structure mattered less in larger, elongated catchments. **I could not resolve the source URL for this from the results; treat it as unverified until located** (it may be a 2023–2025 J. Hydrol./WRR paper).
- Europe: "Analysis of flash-flood runoff response, with examples from major European events" (Borga/Marchi group per result; authors unverified) — [ResearchGate 257926282](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257926282_Analysis_of_Flash-Flood_Runoff_Response_with_Examples_from_Major_European_Events)

**Event-based rainfall shape classification**
- Huff (1967): the quartile scheme classes storms by which quarter of the duration holds the greatest share of total storm rainfall. Huff separated events with a 6-h rainless period. Later work showed that changing the minimum inter-event time (MIT) shifts Huff classes differently in wet tropical and arid locations — [Dunkerley-type modified Huff paper, ResearchGate 360658828](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360658828_Huff_quartile_classification_of_rainfall_intensity_profiles_'storm_patterns'_A_modified_approach_employing_an_intensity_threshold) (Huff 1967 itself, WRR 3(4):1007–1019, is **[background — not retrieved; verify]**)
- "Huff quartile classification of rainfall intensity profiles ('storm patterns'): a modified approach employing an intensity threshold" (believed to be Dunkerley, ~2022; authorship unverified) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360658828_Huff_quartile_classification_of_rainfall_intensity_profiles_'storm_patterns'_A_modified_approach_employing_an_intensity_threshold)
- Bonta: "Development and utility of Huff curves for disaggregating precipitation amounts" — [ResearchGate 43261909](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43261909_Development_and_utility_of_Huff_curves_for_disaggregating_precipitation_amounts); "Cumulative storm rainfall distributions: comparison of Huff curves" — [ResearchGate 283611202](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283611202_Cumulative_storm_rainfall_distributions_Comparison_of_Huff_curves)

**Temporal structure changing with warming**
- Wasko & Sharma (2015, Nature Geoscience 8:527–529, doi:10.1038/NGEO2456), "Steeper temporal distribution of rain intensity at higher temperatures within Australian storms": 6-min rain at 79 Australian sites, with fractions within storm bursts scaled against temperature. Higher temperatures gave "a less uniform temporal pattern, with more intense peak precipitation and weaker precipitation during less intense times", regardless of climatic region and season — [UNSW PDF](https://unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/unsw-adobe-websites/hydrology/2023-11-04-Nature-Geoscience_Wasko-and-Sharma_June-2015-(1).pdf); [UniMelb record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1313593-steeper-temporal-distribution-of-rain-intensity-at-higher-temperatures-within-australian-storms)
- Press coverage of that work (about 40,000 storms) says that for a 5 °C rise, flood peaks in a typical medium-sized catchment could rise by 5–20% on average, with about 45% quoted for Darwin (tropical) and about 12% for Sydney. These are press-release figures; check them against the paper — [UNSW newsroom 2015](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2015/06/why-warmer-storms-could-lead-to-more-flooding-than-expected); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/06/150608120131.htm)
- Visser, Wasko, Sharma & Nathan (2023, Journal of Climate), "Changing storm temporal patterns with increasing temperatures across Australia": station trends show events becoming "increasingly front loaded" over six decades. They report a systematic shift to more front-loaded patterns with rising temperature at nearly all durations and intensities, "with the emphasis on shorter duration events in the tropics", together with intensification of embedded bursts. They conclude that hydrological applications must account for nonstationary temporal patterns — [UniMelb record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1797399-changing-storm-temporal-patterns-with-increasing-temperatures-across-australia)

(Wasko, Sharma et al.) — [UniMelb record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1313592-does-storm-duration-modulate-the-extreme-precipitation-temperature-scaling-relationship%3F); PreventionWeb summary on intensifying peak downpours in Australia — [PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/australia-flash-flooding-risks-increase-peak-downpours-intensify-research-shows)

### Inferences
- **The core gap is confirmed, as far as this search reached.** Studies split into (a) model or design-storm sensitivity (Zhu 2018; HESS 2021; J. Hydrol. 2024; Chinese pattern-specific critical rainfall), which show that shape matters for peaks; (b) observational shape climatologies with no flood linkage (Huff/Bonta/Dolšak/Dunkerley; K-means typologies; Wasko/Visser trends); and (c) a few observational rainfall–hydrograph studies that are descriptive, dryland/Mediterranean, or attribution analyses on daily data. None of the results tested, across a large *observed* inventory, whether shape metrics separate flood from non-flood events at equal depth and duration, or whether adding shape improves threshold skill. A Sri Lankan study doing this (for example, shape-conditioned ED thresholds, or logistic/ROC models with depth + duration + antecedent index + Gini/peak-position) would be a clear contribution.
- The physically expected signal is strongest in small, steep, fast-responding catchments and under dry or moderate antecedent states (Zhu 2018; China 140-catchment attribution, unverified). Under saturated monsoon conditions, depth may dominate. A Sri Lankan design should therefore stratify by antecedent state and catchment size, and should not expect shape to matter everywhere.
- The finding that depth and uniformity covary (J. Hydrol. 2025) means shape effects must be tested *conditional on depth/duration* (e.g., partial effects, matched-depth comparisons). Otherwise shape will be confounded with magnitude.
- The Visser et al. tropical emphasis on short-duration front-loading suggests that trend tests on Sri Lankan event shape are worth doing, but transfer from Australian stations to monsoon South Asia is untested.

### Gaps
- No observational large-sample test of hyetograph shape as a control on flash flood *occurrence* was found, in any region, including South/Southeast Asia.
- Alfieri et al. (design-hyetograph selection; likely Alfieri, Laio & Claps 2008, Hydrol. Process.), Dunkerley's broader reviews on intra-event intensity, Nguyen et al., and Wartalska were not retrieved. **[background — verify before citing]**
- Marchi et al. (2010, J. Hydrol., "Characterisation of selected extreme flash floods in Europe") and Gaume et al. (2009) are landmark observational flash flood catalogues. They are relevant for linking rainfall to unit peak discharge but were not retrieved here **[background — verify]**.
- No South Asian analysis of storm temporal pattern trends with temperature (Wasko/Visser style) was found.

## Q4. Sub-daily satellite rainfall (IMERG, GSMaP, CMORPH) for event shape: hyetograph shape and peak timing fidelity

### Takeaway
IMERG and GSMaP broadly reproduce the timing of the diurnal cycle, but with region- and version-dependent phase errors of about 1–4 h (often early in China; GSMaP sometimes late). Hourly detection and intensity skill, especially for extremes, is weak. This means event-scale hyetograph *shape* metrics from satellite rainfall carry substantial, little-quantified uncertainty. No study was found that directly validates Huff class, peak position or Gini concentration of individual events from IMERG/GSMaP against gauges in a tropical island.

### Cited Findings
- Trinidad (tropical island; three gauges, 2020–2021; Springer 2026): IMERG captured the midday peak but spread it over a broader period (about 1 h). GSMaP placed the peak late, at 13:00. IMERG detected substantially more rain events than GSMaP at all temporal scales. This is the closest analogue to Sri Lanka — [Springer s44288-026-00685-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44288-026-00685-1)
- Global hourly diurnal comparison (HESS 2025, 29:4929): GSMaP peaks slightly later than IMERG, at 07:00–09:00 LST and 19:00–21:00 LST — [HESS 2025](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/29/4929/2025/)
- Mainland China: both IMERG and GSMaP peak 2–4 h early — [ResearchGate 368799067](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/368799067_Evaluation_of_GPM-IMERG_Precipitation_Product_at_Multiple_Spatial_and_Sub-Daily_Temporal_Scales_over_Mainland_China)
- Indonesian Maritime Continent (47 gauges): IMERG had limited ability to capture sub-daily and high-intensity precipitation. GSMaP can capture diurnal peak timing even though hourly correlation is low — [ResearchGate 378835059](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378835059_Validating_IMERG_data_for_diurnal_rainfall_analysis_across_the_Indonesian_maritime_continent_using_gauge_observations)
- Philippines, southwest monsoon: GSMaP Gauge-adjusted v07 detected temporal variations over Luzon better than IMERG Final v06 — [Atmos. Res. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169809525000754)
- Hourly GPM product intercomparison of *event properties* (Atmos. Res. 2025): IMERG had lower false alarm ratios and GSMaP higher detection rates — [Atmos. Res. 2025, S0169809525003230](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169809525003230)
- Continental sub-daily evaluation of IMERG V07B (Remote Sensing of Environment 2025) — [ScienceDirect S003442572500094X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S003442572500094X) (numbers not retrieved)
- Quasi-global IMERG vs GSMaP over land with gauges — [ResearchGate 338652934](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338652934_Quasi-Global_Evaluation_of_IMERG_and_GSMaP_Precipitation_Products_over_Land_Using_Gauge_Observations); arid sub-daily GPM/GSMaP evaluation calls for more sub-daily gauge records — [Remote Sens. 11:2840](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11232840)
- Extremes: in the Xijiang basin, more than half of ≥50 mm/day events were missed by satellite products — [Atmos. Res. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169809523000704)
- Sources stress that findings contradict one another across region, product version (IMERG V06/V07; GSMaP v6–v8) and scale, so it is unclear whether IMERG overstates diurnal features or GSMaP understates them (search synthesis across the above; see e.g. [HESS 2025](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/29/4929/2025/))

### Inferences
- Peak-timing errors of 1–4 h are similar in size to the duration of short flash-flood-producing bursts. Satellite-derived *peak position* (front/back-loading) for short events is therefore likely unreliable. Coarser shape metrics (e.g., Huff quartile on events ≥12–24 h, or Gini concentration on 3-h aggregates) may be more robust. Validating these event-shape metrics directly against Sri Lankan sub-daily gauges is itself a publishable gap.
- The Philippines result (GSMaP-Gauge better than IMERG for temporal variation) and the Trinidad result (GSMaP late peak, IMERG more events) disagree in direction. A Sri Lankan study should test both products, and preferably IMERG V07 Final and GSMaP Gauge v8.

### Gaps
- No study found that evaluates event-level hyetograph shape classes (Huff/peak position/Gini) from IMERG/GSMaP/CMORPH against gauges.
- No Sri Lanka-specific sub-daily IMERG/GSMaP timing evaluation was retrieved in this session.
- CMORPH sub-daily timing results were not retrieved.
